What you are being asked

Each crop is shown twice. On the left is the image as it appears in the paper. On the right the same image carries a red overlay marking every pixel the software read as clipped, meaning it sat at the very top of the brightness range for a bright band on a dark field, or at the very bottom for a dark band on a light one. The white and black ring marks where the software places the band's centre.

The software has already measured how much of the band is clipped. You are not being asked to estimate that. You are being asked whether the software found the right object, which is the one thing it cannot check itself. Four questions per crop, and there is no penalty for saying you could not judge one.

clipped pixels band centre

What each question means

How many bands are in this crop?
More than one band means the box caught two lanes or two stacked bands. Those are excluded from the sample rather than scored, so marking it is all that is needed.
Does the red overlay sit on the band itself?
Answer about the band only. Red elsewhere in the crop is not what this asks about. The red marks pixels the software read as clipped, at the top of the intensity range for a bright band on a dark field and at the bottom for a dark band on a light one. The question is only whether it landed on the band. Red on the background, on a marker, on text, or on a neighbouring band is the software getting it wrong, which is exactly what this round is for.
Ignoring the red, does the band itself look blown out?
Answer from the picture, not from the overlay. A band that was saturated when it was photographed can reach publication with no pixel left at the top of the range, because brightness adjustment, JPEG compression and downsampling all move a flat plateau off the ceiling. Those bands still look blown out: a flat, featureless, evenly bright core with no visible gradient toward the middle. Marking them is how the gap between what the overlay can see and what actually happened gets measured.
Could you judge this crop?
Say no when the band is too faint to be sure it is there, or the crop is too degraded to read. This is a real answer, not a failure, and the rate of it is reported.
